What is Polyendocrine Metabolic Ovarian Syndrome (PMOS)?

PMOS represents a paradigm shift — a holistic diagnostic and therapeutic model that expands the 2003 Rotterdam criteria. It integrates ovarian dysfunction with central insulin resistance, adipose tissue endocrinopathy, low-grade chronic inflammation, and neuroendocrine dysregulation. Unlike classic PCOS which often focuses on reproductive features, PMOS emphasizes metabolic phenotype clusters (insulin-dominant, adrenal-dominant, inflammatory, and mixed).


Dr. Deepa Chudal adopts the International PMOS Consensus 2025-2026, utilizing advanced markers: oral glucose tolerance test with insulin, anti-Mullerian hormone (AMH), hs-CRP, androstenedione, LH/FSH ratio, and metabolic score (TyG index). This allows precise subtyping, early intervention, and prevention of T2DM, NAFLD, and endometrial pathology.


2026 landmark update: New PMOS classification identifies “metabolic predominant” vs “reproductive predominant” — treatment responses differ significantly. Targeted therapy yields 2.5x higher live birth rate vs conventional PCOS protocols.

PMOS Diagnostic Criteria (2026)

  • Menstrual irregularity + hyperandrogenism (clinical/biochemical)
  • Insulin resistance (HOMA-IR >2.5 or TyG index >4.5)
  • Elevated AMH > 4.5 ng/mL + polycystic ovarian morphology
  • Presence of ≥2 metabolic syndrome components
  • Exclusion of thyroid, hyperprolactinemia & NCAH

Phenotyping: A (severe metabolic) | B (classic hyperandrogenic) | C (ovulatory dysfunction predominant) | D (mixed inflammatory)
