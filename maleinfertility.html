Common Causes of Male Factor Infertility

Low Sperm Count (Oligospermia)

Sperm concentration <15 million/mL. Causes: varicocele, infection, hormonal imbalance, genetic factors, lifestyle.

Poor Motility (Asthenozoospermia)

Progressive motility <32%. Associated with infections, antisperm antibodies, oxidative stress, varicocele.

Abnormal Morphology (Teratozoospermia)

Normal forms <4% (Kruger strict). Reduces fertilization potential even with normal count.

DNA Fragmentation

High DFI (>25-30%) linked to recurrent miscarriage and failed IVF. Caused by oxidative stress, varicocele, fever.

Azoospermia (No Sperm in Ejaculate)

Obstructive (blockage) vs non-obstructive (testicular failure). Requires specialized workup.

Varicocele

Present in 40% of infertile men. Correctable cause — microsurgical varicocelectomy improves semen parameters.

Hormonal Imbalances

Low testosterone, high FSH/LH, hyperprolactinemia, thyroid disorders affecting spermatogenesis.

Genetic Causes

Klinefelter syndrome (47,XXY), Y chromosome microdeletions, CFTR mutations (CBAVD).

Comprehensive Male Fertility Diagnostics

Dr. Deepa Chudal offers a complete andrology workup following WHO 2026 guidelines:

  • Semen Analysis (2 samples, 2-4 weeks apart) — Volume, count, motility, morphology, vitality, leukocytes
  • Sperm DNA Fragmentation Index (DFI) — SCSA or TUNEL method
  • Semen Culture & Antibiotic Sensitivity — For leukocytospermia
  • Hormonal Profile — FSH, LH, total testosterone, free testosterone, prolactin, estradiol, TSH
  • Scrotal Doppler Ultrasound — Varicocele detection, testicular size, epididymal abnormalities
  • Genetic Testing — Karyotype, Y chromosome microdeletion, CFTR mutation analysis
  • Testicular Biopsy / Fine Needle Aspiration (FNA) — For azoospermia evaluation

WHO 2026 Semen Reference Ranges

  • Volume: ≥1.5 mL
  • Concentration: ≥16 million/mL (5th percentile)
  • Total count: ≥39 million/ejaculate
  • Total motility: ≥42%
  • Progressive motility: ≥30%
  • Normal morphology: ≥4% (Kruger strict)
  • Vitality: ≥54% live sperm
  • Leukocytes: <1 million/mL
Sample Collection Instructions: 2-5 days abstinence, avoid alcohol/caffeine 48hrs prior, collect in sterile container, deliver to lab within 60 minutes.

Evidence-Based Male Fertility Treatments

Lifestyle Optimization

Smoking cessation, alcohol reduction, weight management, antioxidant-rich diet, and avoiding testicular overheating. Improves parameters in 3-6 months.

Medical Therapy

Antioxidants (CoQ10, zinc, selenium, vitamin C/E, L-carnitine), clomiphene citrate for low testosterone, cabergoline for hyperprolactinemia, antibiotics for infections.

Varicocele Embolization / Surgery

Microsurgical varicocelectomy improves semen parameters in 60-80% of men. Dr. Chudal coordinates with expert urologists.

Intrauterine Insemination (IUI)

For mild-moderate male factor (count >5M post-wash). Sperm washing concentrates motile sperm for uterine placement.

ICSI (Intracytoplasmic Sperm Injection)

Gold standard for severe male factor. Single sperm injected directly into egg — bypasses all natural barriers.

Testosterone Replacement (Carefully!)

Exogenous testosterone suppresses spermatogenesis. Alternative: clomiphene or hCG for hypogonadotropic hypogonadism.

Surgical Sperm Retrieval for Azoospermia & Severe Cases

For men with azoospermia (no sperm in ejaculate) or anejaculation, Dr. Deepa Chudal coordinates with urologists for sperm retrieval procedures:

  • TESA (Testicular Sperm Aspiration) — Needle aspiration from testis. For obstructive azoospermia.
  • PESA (Percutaneous Epididymal Sperm Aspiration) — For epididymal obstruction (congenital bilateral absence of vas deferens).
  • Micro-TESE (Microdissection Testicular Sperm Extraction) — For non-obstructive azoospermia. Microscope-guided extraction — highest yield.
  • Sperm Cryopreservation — Retrieved sperm can be frozen for future ICSI cycles.
Success rates: Sperm retrieval success 90-100% (obstructive), 40-60% (non-obstructive with micro-TESE).

Azoospermia Types

  • Obstructive: Blockage in ducts. Normal spermatogenesis. Causes: CBAVD (CFTR mutation), vasectomy, infection.
  • Non-obstructive: Testicular failure. High FSH, small testes. Causes: Klinefelter, Y chromosome microdeletion, cryptorchidism, chemotherapy.

Pre-procedure genetic counseling and hormonal optimization available.

Lifestyle & Nutritional Tips to Improve Sperm Health

Do's:

  • Eat antioxidant-rich foods (berries, nuts, leafy greens, tomatoes)
  • Take supplements: CoQ10 (200mg), zinc (15mg), selenium (100mcg), vitamin C (500mg), vitamin D
  • Maintain healthy BMI (18.5-24.9)
  • Wear loose-fitting underwear
  • Manage stress (yoga, meditation)

Don'ts:

  • Smoking (reduces count by 20-30%)
  • Excessive alcohol (>2 drinks/day)
  • Hot baths, saunas, laptop on lap
  • Anabolic steroids or testosterone supplements
  • Recreational drugs (marijuana, cocaine)

Sperm production cycle: ~74 days. Lifestyle changes show improvement in 3 months.
